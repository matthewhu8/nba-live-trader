## 3. The four measurement defects (fixed on our branch)

| # | defect | mechanism | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exit-window lookahead | entry price read at `wct + feed_delay_s`, exit search started at `wct` | positions could exit up to 20s **before entering**. 25 of 81 trades held <20s and won **25 for 25** |
| 2 | TP over-crediting | TP exits booked at the tick that breached the threshold, not the resting maker limit at `entry + TP` (PR #50) | 18 of 36 TP exits overshot — median 6¢ past the limit, max 24¢ |
| 3 | Fees hardcoded to zero | `_compute_maker_fees()` returned `0.0` unconditionally | zeroed the taker cost on every stop-out, i.e. every loser |
| 4 | Cents labelled dollars | `net = gross_cents × contracts − fees_dollars` | every figure **100× too large**; dollar fees against a cents quantity made fees look 100× too small, which is why defect 3 looked immaterial |

**Guard added:** `mmoe_backtest.py` now raises on any exit earlier than `wct + feed_delay_s`.
Stated against `wct` rather than the entry anchor so re-anchoring fails loudly. Verified to
fire when the bug is reintroduced.

**Do not assert `hold_time_s >= feed_delay_s`** — once the anchor is correct, hold time is
measured *from* the anchor and legitimate sub-second holds exist.

**Correct fee formula** (CLAUDE.md's table was missing the `P×(1−P)` term):
```
maker: ceil(0.0175 × contracts × P × (1-P) × 100) / 100
taker: ceil(0.07   × contracts × P × (1-P) × 100) / 100
       P = price in dollars (50¢ → 0.50)
```
Entry always maker. Exit maker only on `take_profit`; `stop_loss`, `momentum_flip`,
`garbage_time`, `time_gate` all cross the book and pay taker.

## 4. Matt's model (`origin/main`, PR #51)

### 4.1 Architecture

**58 raw features = 33 physics + 11 pregame + 14 market.** The 14 market features pass
through `nn.Linear(14, 4)` (`MARKET_EMBED_DIM = 4`), so expert input dim is **48**. Three
shared experts, per-head gates (`gate_a/b/c`), ~30K params. `use_market_encoder=False`
bypasses the encoder for ablation.

### 4.2 Checkpoints

| file | epoch | val_loss | expert dim | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mmoe_delay20.pt` | 5 | 0.4241 | 48 | **deployed** |
| `mmoe_delay20_nomktenc.pt` | 5 | 0.4185 | 58 | better val_loss, worse Head B |
| `mmoe_delay20_h360.pt` | 10 | 0.4306 | **83** | old architecture |
| `mmoe_delay20_h600.pt` | 3 | 0.4401 | **83** | old architecture |

Per-head, from `skills/backtesting.md`:

| head | prior (83) | physics-only | + market encoder |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — run classifier (AUCPR) | 0.1593 | **0.1675** | 0.1590 |
| B — price trajectory (dir acc) | 61.1% | 61.3% | **62.5%** |
| C — survival hazard (Brier) | 0.0860 | **0.0848** | 0.0860 |

**Selection is on Head B, not val loss** — correct, since entries are driven by Head B's
trajectory sign. That's why the encoder shipped despite the worse total. `h360`/`h600` are the
**old 83-feature architecture** and cannot be compared to the current model.

### 4.3 What it was trained on — and why every metric is provisional

Derived from `mmoe_scaler_delay20.pkl` (58 features, fit on 341,472 rows):

| | old 83-feat model | new 58-feat model |
|---|---|---|
| rows fit | 350,449 | 341,472 |
| joint (possession + tick) rows | 19,811 (5.7%) | **18,764 (5.5%)** |
| `has_pregame_data` mean | 0.4356 | **0.4352** |
| `lineup_confidence` mean | — | **0.1486** → real lineup samples on ~6% of rows |

Four problems, all still present:

1. **Head B trained on ~18.7K rows, not the documented ~42K.** Docs give three mutually
   inconsistent figures (`49,576`, `~42K`, `24K`). The scaler is authoritative. After
   `trainer.py`'s mask (`has_market_data × (|traj| > 0.02)`) the effective sample is ~12K.

2. **The split is 40/60, not 80/20.** If the joint total is 49,576, splitting at
   `HEADB_SPLIT_DATE = 2026-04-07` leaves 19,810 train / 29,766 val. `dataset.py:50` claims
   "~80/20". Reported metrics were measured on a val set *larger* than the train set.

3. **Trained before the `wall_clock_ts` repair.** Fingerprint: market `yes_bid` std is 35.25
   (new) and 35.92 (old), while repaired in-band ticks give 11.63. A std of 35 requires heavy
   mass at both 1¢ and 99¢ — the settled-price signature. The new scaler is statistically
   indistinguishable from the old one on every market feature.

4. **55% of Head B's validation rows were corrupt.** Corruption tracks late tip-offs, which
   cluster after the split date. Of the 1,869 corrupt *training* rows, **zero** had pregame
   data — so corruption did not damage what the model learned. It damaged **early stopping,
   best-epoch selection, and every reported metric.** The deployed checkpoint stopped at
   epoch 5, which is suspiciously early on a val set that noisy.

## 10. Method cautions — things that burned us today

- **Never estimate a fix by filtering.** Dropping sub-20s trades from the biased run suggested
  −$2,600; actually re-running gave +$62. A corrected run re-simulates those positions and the
  overlap guard then admits a different trade set. Filtering a biased output is not a fix.
- **Use `scale_`, never `sqrt(var_)`.** Cost me a 232σ claim that was really 8.6σ.
- **Check whether a "bug" was already fixed before quoting its magnitude.** The `sub_count`
  drift I reported as 12–15σ was 0.6σ; my sample pooled sessions from before and after a
  2026-05-13 fix.
- **When a doc-stated invariant disagrees with code output, reconcile it.** CLAUDE.md's 55.7%
  break-even had been correct all along while the backtest's P&L was 100× off. The two
  disagreed for months and nobody reconciled them.
- **Guards must be tested in both directions.** Verify a new invariant *fires* when you
  reintroduce the bug, not just that it passes.
- **A silent zero is the most dangerous value in this codebase.** Missing pregame rows, dead
  cache columns, `.get(k, 0.0)` misses and swallowed exceptions all produce plausible-looking
  zeros that no test catches.
